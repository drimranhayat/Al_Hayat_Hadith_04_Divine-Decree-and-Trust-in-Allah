# Topic 004 source and editorial review

40 unique publisher IDs; each has Arabic, published English and published Urdu material and a publisher Authentic assessment. The eighty API response files are retained unchanged and hashed by content-manifest.json. Direct primary references and the publisher reference metadata are included in reference-register.csv and reference-provenance.json.

The learning bank has 80 Q&A and 66 MCQs. Each question carries a source field and exact anchor. Unique stems, answer-choice balance and per-option feedback are checked. These checks establish traceability, not independent scholarly certification or exhaustive coverage of every chain and legal application.

Added Arabic vowel marks are held separately and checked against the source's underlying letters. The source's Arabic explanations/vocabulary remain in raw records and are linked rather than shown unvowelled. Vowel accuracy has no independent specialist sign-off.

A missing Urdu translation for candidate 65772 caused its exclusion; entry 29 instead uses the authenticated and published multilingual record 3701. Alternate IDs 66513/65037, 66522/4811 and 66538/4721 were not counted twice. Cross-topic reuse is logged explicitly.

Entry 33 follows HadeethEnc record 11203, which names Umar al-Jumai. Ahmad 17217 is identified in the linked Dorar takhrij record, which also permits examination of the original wording. The interface notes the naming/chain distinction; the publisher's report assessment is not represented as independent soundness of every route.

No complete isnad, translation revision number or specialist approval has been invented.

Entry 15 cites Bukhari 2291, whose displayed opening attributes the report to al-Layth rather than setting out a full continuous chain at that point. Its dedicated note identifies this feature; publisher authentication is retained without an invented connected chain.
